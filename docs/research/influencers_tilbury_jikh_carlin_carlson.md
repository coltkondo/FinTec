## 6. How the app should encode these creators

### 6.1 Design rules
1. **Separate principles from commentary and picks.** Each record has a `type`: `principle` (durable rule), `framework` (a procedure such as a DCF or quadrant), `pick` (a dated ticker view), `commentary` (macro/sentiment), or `product_mention` (affiliate/sponsor).
2. **Every `pick` and `commentary` record expires.** It needs `as_of` and `stale_after_days` (picks: 120; commentary: 60). Stale items show a warning badge.
3. **Show conflict-of-interest flags** next to any platform, broker, or product mention (`sponsored: true`, `creator_owned_product: true`).
4. **Evidence alignment rating**, cross-referenced to the academic KB by `evidence_refs` (paper IDs):
   - `A` = strong support
   - `B` = mixed or conditional
   - `C` = weak, or contradicted on average
   - `D` = contradicts the evidence for this user profile

### 6.5 Facts the four creators leave out that this user needs (added in fact-check, 2026-11-10)
All four creators are thin on current US mechanics. Store these as `type: "reference_fact"` with `as_of` and refresh them yearly.

- **2026 contribution limits [V, IRS IR-2025-111]:**
  - 401(k)/403(b)/457/TSP employee deferral: **$24,500**. Catch-up at age 50+: $8,000. Ages 60–63: $11,250.
  - IRA (Roth plus Traditional combined): **$7,500**. Catch-up at 50+: $1,100, now inflation-indexed.
  - Roth IRA phase-out (MAGI): **$153,000–$168,000 single; $242,000–$252,000 MFJ**. Above that, consider a backdoor Roth (watch the pro-rata rule if you hold pre-tax IRA money).
  - Employer match does **not** count toward the $24,500 employee limit.
- **0% long-term capital gains / qualified dividend bracket, 2026:** taxable income up to $49,450 single / $98,900 MFJ [S, Rev. Proc. 2025-32]. This matters for asset location: hold dividend-heavy funds (SCHD) in IRAs rather than taxable if you are above the bracket.
- **Cash-yield snapshot (re-fetch at runtime; do not hard-code):**
  - US Treasury bills on 2026-11-10 (coupon-equivalent): 4-week 3.94%, 13-week 4.22%, 26-week 4.41%, 52-week 4.54%. [V, Treasury daily bill rates]
  - FDIC national average savings rate: **0.38%**; national rate cap 4.63% (table dated 2026-10-19). [V, FDIC] A large-bank 0.01–0.40% savings account is therefore costing this user several hundred dollars a year on ~$20K, compared with a competitive HYSA or T-bill.
  - I bonds: composite **5.51%** (fixed 1.40%) for issues 2026-11-01 to 2027-04-30. Limit $10,000 electronic per SSN per calendar year. Locked for 12 months; lose the last 3 months of interest if redeemed before 5 years. Electronic only since 2025-01-01, so the paper "tax refund" route is gone. [V, TreasuryDirect] **Not suitable for the first months of an emergency fund** because of the 12-month lock.
- **Tax treatment of cash options:**
  - HYSA and CD interest is fully taxable (federal plus state).
  - Treasury bill and I-bond interest is **exempt from state and local income tax**.
  - Treasury money-market funds (e.g., government MMFs holding mostly Treasuries) pass through partial state exemption depending on state rules.
  - After-tax yield = yield × (1 − fed rate − state rate) for HYSA, vs. yield × (1 − fed rate) for T-bills. The app should compute both.
- **Insurance boundary:**
  - FDIC/NCUA covers $250K per depositor, per insured institution, per ownership category.
  - Brokerage cash and money-market funds are covered by SIPC (custody failure up to $500K, including $250K cash), **not** against market loss, and an MMF is not a bank deposit.
  - Fintech "banking-as-a-service" apps add a middleware failure risk (Synapse, 2024).
